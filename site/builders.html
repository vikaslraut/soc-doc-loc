02
Who does what

The line the law draws, respected precisely

Most handovers get messy here. The law draws a clear line between what a builder can give and what the society's first managing committee must do.

You are the rightful source of the construction and formation documents. Share certificates are not yours to issue. They are the statutory duty of the first managing committee, and the share register, nominations and minutes follow from them. soc-doc-loc lets you load everything that's yours to give, and hands the committee a structured framework for their part, left empty where it should be empty.

03
One list, five laws

The Builder Handover Checklist: 28 items and where each comes from in law

The documents a new society must hold come from five different sources: RERA 2016, MOFA 1963, Model Bye-law 89, the 2018 deemed-conveyance GR, and the fire and lift Acts. No one publishes a single list, and MahaRERA has no official handover checklist. soc-doc-loc does.

Each of the 28 items shows the provision that requires it. Tick it off, attach the file, add notes ("asked twice, promised by March"), or mark a conditional item "not applicable" with a reason. Progress and a grade update as you go, section by section.

05
The first link

You are the start of the chain of title

Every flat's chain of ownership starts with the Index II between you and the allottee: document number, registration date, agreement value, market value, stamp duty and registration fee. As each registration happens during sales, the sub-registrar's record goes straight into that flat's vault, attributed to you correctly.

By handover the society holds a complete, verified set. When the first resale happens, often before the society is fully formed, your original sale stays at the root, marked as such, with the new owner above it. You are never the weak link in the chain of title, and the "can you send us the original agreement?" calls stop.

Stop triple printing

Do it once, not three times

Here's what happens today. At possession your team prints each buyer's possession and parking letters, makes a copy for the society and keeps a master set. That's the same work three times, and the society's copy is the one that goes missing.

With soc-doc-loc you upload each document once and tag it to the flat. It is the buyer's record, the society's record and the permanent archive all at once. Possession letters, parking letters, KYC and loan NOCs are yours to issue, and you issue them once.

Quality control, flat by flat

A self-checking handover scorecard for every flat

The Data Scorecard runs 25 statutory checks on every flat, all the time. Is the Index II on file? Is the sale value within 25% of market value? Is stamp duty at least 6%? Are the possession letter, parking letter and KYC on file? You don't need someone ticking a checklist 200 times.

It also separates your duties from theirs. Everything you were responsible for shows green. What's flagged, such as "no nomination filed", belongs to the members and the committee. Members see their own flat's gaps and send the documents to the society office, not to you. Your phone stops ringing.

Theirs to fill, yours to enable

A working framework for the committee, ready on day one

You don't issue share certificates or admit nominations. You do hand over a system where those jobs take minutes instead of months. Each flat has a ready slot for its share certificate, linked to the right member, shares and flat. When the committee issues and signs a certificate, they scan and attach it, and the share register fills itself in.

The share register shows exactly which certificates are still to be issued, flat by flat: a punch-list for the committee's first task. The nomination register is ready too, with the 100% rule enforced and the statutory Appendix-14 form built in. A new committee can get compliant in weeks rather than letting it slide for years.

Upload them once and the society inherits a complete archive. Nobody has to ask "do you still have a copy of the OC?" three years later.

The founding general body meeting and the appointment of the provisional committee are effectively convened by you. Record that meeting in the proceeding book, and the society's minutes start properly from day one instead of with an empty book.

This one protects you

Tamper-proof proof of exactly what you handed over

Handover disputes are common. A year or two later, a society claims the builder never handed over a document or got a figure wrong. Every change in soc-doc-loc is recorded: what, who, when and why. The record is read-only and secured by a hash chain, so history can't be quietly altered.

That gives you a time-stamped record of exactly what you handed over, fixed at the moment ownership passed to the society. It separates what you authored from what the committee did afterwards. Committees will change through elections, and your handover record stays intact through all of them. It protects you long after you've left the project.
