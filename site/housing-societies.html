Who owns what, since when

Chain of ownership, preserved across every resale

Open a flat and its Index II is right there: document number, registration date, market value, sale value, stamp duty, registration fee and share capital, with the owner and co-owners named on the deed.

When a flat is resold, nothing is erased or overwritten. The new owner appears on top, and the previous owner stays below, marked ceased on the transfer date. A committee five years from now won't have to piece together who owned which flat from handwritten ledgers.

08
The register auditors check hardest

Share register and share ledger that check themselves

The Share Register (Bye-law 14, Rule 60) shows flat, certificate number, share serials, value, member and joint holders. It also tells you which certificates have been scanned and uploaded, and which are still missing, so you can see the gaps and close them one by one.

The Share Ledger (Bye-law 9, Rule 65) is the full history: every holding and transfer, current and ceased, with capital collected and returned and the net balance the society holds.

09
The register that protects families

Nominations that add up, and stay on record

Nominations are among the most sensitive registers a society keeps, and among the most neglected. Record the nomination date, the date the managing committee admitted it, each nominee's details and relationship, and their share. The system insists the shares total 100%, as the bye-law requires.

The signed Appendix-14 form (Bye-law 31) is attached to the record. The Nomination Register (Model Bye-laws 25 to 27) shows every nomination at a glance, flags minor nominees and prints in the format auditors expect, including revocations.

When a member passes away and the nomination is missing or wrong, families can be torn apart and the society can be caught up in disputes for years. Keeping this register accurate protects members' families.

10
Hold it, minute it, prove it

A proceeding book for AGM, SGM and committee meetings

Record each meeting with its date, venue, chairperson, quorum, member attendance, non-member attendees and authorities present, resolutions with proposer and seconder, running notes and the scanned, signed minutes. The register is batched by financial year with page numbers, and prints as a statutory Proceeding Book.

The bye-laws require these meetings to be held and minuted for a society to stay Class A. Here the count is tracked automatically, so a missed meeting or unsigned minutes cannot slip by unnoticed.

12
Funds and the people who run them

Investment register and managing committee register

Model Bye-law 140(8) lists the Investment Register among the books a society must keep, and investing funds without the general body's permission is an offence. Track every FD and instrument: bank, folio, rate, maturity, interest earned each year, TDS deducted, and what was realised. Sinking fund, repair fund and surplus are all visible in one place.

The managing committee register records who held office, in which role and for which term, so the society's leadership history is never in doubt after an election.
